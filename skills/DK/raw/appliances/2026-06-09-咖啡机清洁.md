[4] Cleaning Your Coffee Maker After Each Use

[5] Deep Cleaning Your Coffee Maker

[6] Coffee makers are convenient household appliances that can provide you with a fresh cup of java first thing in the morning. However, due to the high levels of moisture, they can easily harbor mold and bacteria. Drip filter coffee makers require cleaning after each use. They also need to be deep cleaned monthly to remove hard water deposits, leftover coffee oils, and other impurities. With a little elbow grease, you can keep your coffee maker nice and clean.

[7] Wash the removable parts with warm, soapy water and a dish rag.

[8] Wipe down the outside of the machine with a soapy rag, then again with a clean one.

[9] Reassemble the machine after all the parts are completely dry.

[10] 1 Disassemble the coffee maker. Remove any parts that come off, such as the pot, the brew basket, and any other removable parts. These part should be cleaned separately from the rest of the coffee maker.[1] X Research source

[11] 2 Wash the removable parts. Submerge the removable parts in warm, soapy water, and wash them using a dish rag. You can wash removable parts of your coffee maker the same way you would wash dishes.[2] X Research source Glass pots can be very breakable. When washing your coffee maker's pot, treat the glass gingerly. Some coffee makers may have parts that can be washed in a dishwasher. Check the instructions manual, if you still have it. If you're unsure if your coffee maker can be washed in the dishwasher, err on the side of caution and hand wash it. Advertisement

[14] 3 Wipe down the outside of the machine. Take a wet, soapy rag and give the outside of the machine a good wipe down. Wipe down the sides and remove any coffee grinds from the warming plate. When you're down, wipe off any soap suds with a clean, damp rag.[3] X Research source

[15] 4 Reassemble your machine. After all the parts are dry, you can put your coffee machine back together. It is ready to use again. Make a habit of cleaning your coffee maker like this after each use.

[16] 1 Add a vinegar and water solution to your coffee maker.[4] X Expert Source Jonathan TavarezCleaning Specialist Expert Interview Make a mixture using 2 parts warm water and 1 part white vinegar.[5] X Expert Source Marcus ShieldsHouse Cleaning Professional Expert Interview The precise amounts will vary depending on the size of your coffee maker, but make roughly enough to fill the reservoir. Pour the mixture into the reservoir like you normally would with water when making coffee.[6] X Research source

[17] 2 Let the coffee machine run half a brew cycle.[7] X Expert Source Jonathan TavarezCleaning Specialist Expert Interview Turn the coffee machine on. Keep an eye on it as it brews. Once about half the pot is full with the filtered vinegar/water mixture, turn the pot off.[8] X Research source

[18] 3 Let the coffee maker sit for an hour. Set a timer for an hour. Let the coffee pot sit for this time frame.[9] X Research source This will give the vinegar time to sit in the machine and clean out any mold.

[19] 4 Complete the brew cycle. After letting the coffee maker sit for an hour, turn the machine back on. Let it run the rest of the brew cycle.[10] X Expert Source Jonathan TavarezCleaning Specialist Expert Interview When done, all the vinegar/water mixture should be filtered into the pot. You can pour this mixture down the sink.[11] X Research source

[20] 5 Run three brew cycles with plain water. Fill your coffee pot with clean water. Pour this into the reservoir and run a complete brew cycle.[12] X Expert Source Jonathan TavarezCleaning Specialist Expert Interview When it's done, pour the water out and run two more brew cycles with clean water. This should clean any vinegar out of your coffee maker.[13] X Research source

[21] 6 Wipe down the coffee maker. After disinfecting the coffee maker, take a clean, damp rag. Wipe down the exterior of the machine to remove any dirt and debris. You should also make sure to remove coffee grinds from the warming plate.[14] X Research source

[24] 2 Leave the reservoir lid open. Many people keep the reservoir lid closed. However, it's a good idea to leave it open when not in use. This will help dry out the machine, preventing mold and bacteria from growing in a moist environment.

[25] 3 Rinse the brew basket in the sink regularly. The brew basket is very susceptible to mold as it gets wet during the brewing process. Make sure you rinse out the brew basket after each use and make a habit of cleaning it each time you do dishes.
